Troubleshooting

Wobbly rockets, Kraken attacks, and other common disasters, diagnosed and fixed.

On this page

  • I lost control of my probe

  • Engines flamed out unexpectedly
  • My docking approach won't behave

KSP fails loudly and specifically. Almost every "disaster" has a small number of common causes, and once you can recognize the symptom you can usually fix it in under a minute. This page works as a lookup: find the thing that just happened to you, read the likely causes, apply the fix.

My rocket flips during ascent

Symptom

The rocket is climbing fine, then suddenly tumbles end-over-end, usually somewhere in the 30-60km range or right after a hard turn, and no amount of SAS or manual input brings it back under control.

This is aerodynamic, not structural — your rocket's center of pressure has ended up ahead of its center of mass while moving fast enough for air to matter, so any small deviation from prograde gets amplified instead of damped.

Likely causeFix
Going too fast, too low, still deep in thick atmosphereThrottle back slightly through max-Q (roughly 10-13km) instead of holding full throttle the whole way up.
Turning too sharply during the gravity turnStart the turn earlier and make it gradual — a few degrees at a time rather than one aggressive pitch-over.
Insufficient fin authority at the tailAdd or upsize control surfaces/fins at the back of the rocket to pull the center of pressure aft of the center of mass.
Asymmetric design (uneven radial parts, off-center payload)Check symmetry in the VAB; an unevenly loaded rocket wants to rotate even with perfect flying.
SAS is a bandage, not a cure

If SAS is fighting hard to hold prograde, that's a sign the underlying aerodynamic design is unstable, not that you need a stronger reaction wheel. Fix the fins/CoM balance first — SAS should only be nudging, not wrestling.

My rocket wobbles violently under thrust

Symptom

The rocket visibly flexes or oscillates along its length under thrust, sometimes feeding back into a worsening wobble until something snaps off.

This is a joint-rigidity problem: KSP's part connections have some give, and long stacks or heavy off-axis parts can turn that give into a visible, growing oscillation.

Likely causeFix
Long, unsupported stack of tanksBreak the stack up with struts, or use fewer/larger tanks instead of many small stacked ones.
Heavy parts attached radially (boosters, landers, rovers)Strut them directly to the core stack rather than relying on the single attachment node.
Too few struts overall on a tall rocketAdd physical struts between stages, especially across decouplers, which are naturally floppy joints.

Autostrut settings, explained

Right-clicking most parts in the editor exposes an Autostrut option, which rigidly pins that part's position relative to another part without needing a visible strut model. There are three modes worth knowing:

  • Grandparent part — autostruts to the part two levels up the part tree from this one. Good general-purpose choice for parts partway up a stack, since it distributes rigidity along the tree without everything anchoring to one single point.
  • Heaviest part — autostruts to whichever part on the vessel currently has the most mass, which is often a fuel tank low in the stack. Useful for stabilizing parts that need to stay rigid relative to the craft's main mass, like landing legs or heavy radial boosters.
  • Root part — autostruts directly to the vessel's root part. The most aggressive option; great for keeping a whole tall rocket rigid, but can look physically odd (parts refusing to flex at all) and won't survive if the root part itself is later decoupled away.
Reducing part count also helps

Every joint is a potential wobble source. A rocket built from fewer, larger parts is inherently stiffer than the same design built from many small parts chained together, independent of any strutting you add.

Everything exploded for no reason (the Kraken)

Symptom

Your craft violently disassembles itself with no collision, no overheating, and no obvious player error — parts fly off at absurd speeds, or the whole vessel is destroyed instantly.

"The Kraken" is the community's nickname for physics-engine glitches that cause this kind of uncommanded, physically nonsensical destruction. It isn't a single bug — it's a family of edge cases in how KSP's physics simulation handles extreme situations. Common triggers:

  • Excessive part clipping — parts overlapping heavily in the editor can suddenly "resolve" that overlap violently once physics loads in.
  • Extreme timewarp while under thrust, or while docked to something under load — physics warp keeps the simulation running at a multiplier it wasn't designed to handle smoothly, and joints or colliders can snap.
  • Very high part counts feeding a wobble back on itself — see the section above; enough unstable joints compounding each other can spiral into a full Kraken event rather than just a visible wiggle.
Mitigation
  • Avoid timewarping while your engines are firing — throttle down to zero before warping, and only warp under thrust in short, cautious increments if you must.
  • Save on-rails (non-physics) warp for coasting between burns; it's perfectly stable at any multiplier since physics isn't being simulated during it.
  • Keep an eye on part count and clipping on very large stations or ships — if a design is borderline wobbly at low part count, doubling it up rarely goes well.

I lost control of my probe

Symptom

The probe stops responding to input — SAS won't engage, staging doesn't fire, or the control indicator shows the craft as uncontrolled.

Likely causeFix
Dead battery / no electric chargeCheck the electric charge readout — an unpowered probe core can't process commands. Add solar panels, RTGs, or fuel cells, and manage power draw during eclipses.
Lost CommNet signalCheck your CommNet connection in map view — a probe with no relay path back to Kerbin (or no direct antenna range) loses most command capability. Add a stronger antenna or route through a relay satellite.

A probe with a pilot-equivalent onboard (some advanced probe cores) can retain partial autonomous control even without signal, but most basic probe cores need an active connection for anything beyond holding SAS.

My spacecraft overheated or a part exploded on reentry

Symptom

Parts start flashing orange/white on the way down through atmosphere, then explode, sometimes taking the whole craft with them.

Likely causeFix
Reentry angle too steep or too fastAim for a shallower entry — a lower, more grazing periapsis bleeds speed over a longer path instead of dumping all the heat at once.
Missing or undersized heat shieldAdd a heat shield with enough ablator for the reentry speed involved (interplanetary returns need much more ablator than a simple Kerbin orbital reentry).
Wrong orientation during entryPresent a blunt, heat-shielded end first rather than a pointy or unshielded side — the shield is only protecting whatever it's actually facing into the airflow.

Engines flamed out unexpectedly

Symptom

An engine cuts out mid-burn even though the stage should still have fuel left, or fuel gauges show remaining propellant that the engine isn't drawing on.

Likely causeFix
Genuinely out of fuel or oxidizer in that stage's tanksCheck the VAB Δv readout before launch so you know how much margin the stage actually has.
Fuel flow/priority issues between tanksCheck tank fuel-flow priority in the right-click menu — fuel can be trapped in a tank that isn't feeding the active engine if priorities or crossfeed are misconfigured.
Asparagus staging fuel duct misconfigurationConfirm fuel ducts/lines are pointed the right direction (feeding inward toward the core) and that the outer tanks are set to drain before the core empties.

My docking approach won't behave

Symptom

The craft won't hold still, drifts unpredictably, or SAS seems to be actively fighting your attempts to line up with the target port.

Likely causeFix
SAS in the wrong modeMake sure you're using a target-relative SAS hold (target prograde/retrograde, or point-at-target) rather than a plain orbital hold, which won't track a moving target.
RCS not enabledToggle RCS on (R) — without it, fine translation is impossible and you'll be fighting the main engine or reaction wheels alone.
Navball in the wrong reference modeSwitch the navball to Target mode so velocity readouts reflect your motion relative to the target, not relative to the body you're orbiting.

For the full step-by-step approach procedure, see the Rendezvous & Docking page.

The game glitched on scene load / craft is stuck in the ground

Symptom

A vessel loads partially clipped into terrain or the launchpad, jitters violently, or is destroyed the instant a scene finishes loading — before you've done anything at all.

This one is a known engine quirk rather than a design mistake on your part — physics colliders sometimes haven't fully settled the instant a scene loads, especially on complex craft or busy scenes like a crowded launchpad. It's usually not worth debugging further. The fast fix is simply:

  1. Revert to launch from the pause menu if you're still early in the flight.
  2. Reload a recent quicksave with F9 if reverting isn't available.
  3. Use the recovery option on a badly stuck vessel if neither of the above applies, then relaunch.
The best general-purpose defense: quicksave aggressively

Every single failure mode on this page is far less costly if you were quicksaving along the way. Get in the habit of hitting F5 before every risky maneuver, staging event, or atmospheric entry, and F9 the instant something goes wrong — see the full key reference on the Keyboard Shortcuts page.
