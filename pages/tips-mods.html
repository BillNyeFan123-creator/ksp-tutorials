Good habits

None of these are complicated, but they compound. A player who does all five consistently will lose far fewer ships to their own mistakes than one who doesn't.

  1. Quicksave constantly. Hit F5 before every risky maneuver, staging event, docking attempt, or atmospheric entry. F9 reloads it instantly if things go wrong. See the full key reference on Keyboard Shortcuts.
  2. Build in stages you understand. Don't copy a huge design from a video or the internet before you understand why each part is there — build up complexity gradually so you can actually debug problems when they show up.
  3. Check the VAB Δv and TWR readout before every launch. Confirming your rocket can physically do the mission before you light the engines is much cheaper than discovering it can't halfway to orbit.
  4. Name your vessels and craft files descriptively. "Mun Lander Mk3" is worth more than "Untitled Space Craft" the moment you have a dozen active missions in the Tracking Station.
  5. Use Revert to Launch liberally. Available from the pause menu shortly after launching, it lets you retry a badly-designed rocket without wasting funds in Career mode — there's no shame in reverting a dozen times while iterating on a new design.

Pitfalls to avoid

Mistakes that span the whole game, not just building
  • High TWR, no Δv. A rocket can leap off the pad impressively and still not carry enough fuel to actually reach orbit. TWR tells you if you can lift off; Δv tells you if you can finish the job. Check both.
  • Turning too hard on ascent. An aggressive gravity turn induces aerodynamic instability and can flip the rocket — see Troubleshooting for the full fix.
  • Forgetting a probe core. A crewed pod with no backup probe core means the whole craft goes uncontrolled if the crew is lost or transferred out, and an uncrewed stage with no probe core at all can't be controlled after separation.
  • Wrong-order staging. Double-check the staging list before launch — a decoupler firing before its engine finishes, or an engine igniting before its fairing separates, are both classic and entirely preventable failures.
  • No CommNet coverage for a distant probe. A probe sent past the edge of your relay network loses most control the moment it's out of range — plan relay coverage before you commit to the transfer burn.
  • Landing on a slope with a narrow stance. Landing legs placed close together make a top-heavy lander easy to tip over on anything but flat ground. Widen the stance or lower the center of mass for surface landers.
  • Committing to an aerobrake without checking a survivable periapsis. Too low and the craft burns up or breaks apart; too high and it doesn't capture at all. Check the numbers before you commit.
  • Running out of return fuel on a landed mission. Budget Δv for the full round trip — ascent back to orbit and the return transfer — not just the trip out and the landing.
  • Forgetting a heat shield. Any craft returning from orbit (especially from another body) needs ablator-rated protection sized for the reentry speed involved; see Troubleshooting.

Useful mods

All optional — stock KSP is fully playable without any of these, but they smooth out rough edges once you're past the very first hours.

  • Kerbal Engineer Redux (KER) — detailed Δv, TWR, and burn-time readouts in the VAB and in flight, broken down per stage. Removes the guesswork from "will this actually get me there."
  • MechJeb — an autopilot and information mod. Most useful once you already understand manual flying: it's excellent for double-checking your own numbers or automating a repetitive maneuver you've already done by hand a dozen times.
  • Docking Port Alignment Indicator — overlays a precise alignment gauge for docking approaches, showing exact offset and rotation relative to the target port.
  • Transfer Window Planner — shows upcoming interplanetary transfer windows and their approximate Δv cost, so you're not hunting for a launch window blind.
  • Kerbal Alarm Clock — sets alarms for maneuver nodes, SOI changes, and transfer windows so long timewarps don't blow past the moment you needed to act.
CKAN: the standard mod manager

CKAN is the community-standard mod manager for KSP. It handles dependency versions and compatibility checks for you, rather than requiring you to install and update every mod's files by hand.

Want more challenge? Life-support mods

Mods like Kerbal Health, USI Life Support, and TAC Life Support add an extra survival layer — food, water, oxygen, or long-duration health effects — that stock KSP doesn't track. They're a popular way to add stakes to long-duration bases and interplanetary missions once the core game feels too forgiving. See the Bases & Colonies page for where this matters most.

Glossary

Every acronym and piece of jargon used across this site, in one place.

TermMeaning
Δv (delta-v)A budget of achievable velocity change; the "currency" spent on every burn, maneuver, and landing.
TWRThrust-to-weight ratio; must exceed 1 (locally) for a craft to lift off under its own power.
Ap / PeApoapsis (the highest point of an orbit) and periapsis (the lowest point).
SOISphere of Influence — the region around a body where its gravity dominates a craft's trajectory.
SASStability Assist System; holds or auto-orients a craft's heading.
RCSReaction Control System; small thrusters used for fine translation, especially during docking.
CommNetThe signal-relay network that determines whether a probe has a working control link.
ISRUIn-Situ Resource Utilization; converting mined Ore into usable fuel on-site instead of launching it from Kerbin.
OreThe raw resource harvested from a body's surface and refined via ISRU into fuel.
AblatorThe consumable heat-shield material that burns away to protect a craft during reentry.
Ejection burn / angleThe burn (and its timing angle relative to a body) that sends a craft out of a body's SOI on an interplanetary trajectory.
Transfer windowThe periodic time window when two bodies' relative positions make an efficient transfer between them possible.
Hohmann transferThe classic two-burn, minimum-energy orbit transfer between two circular orbits.
Bi-elliptic transferA three-burn transfer that can beat a Hohmann transfer's Δv cost for very large orbit-size changes.
Gravity assistUsing a body's gravity during a flyby to change a craft's speed or direction for free.
Oberth effectThe principle that a burn produces more effective Δv the faster (and thus deeper in a gravity well) you're already moving when you make it.
BiomeA distinct surface region on a body that counts separately for science experiment results.
KerbinThe homeworld, roughly Earth-equivalent in role.
MunKerbin's larger, airless moon — the traditional first landing target.
MinmusKerbin's smaller, low-gravity moon, known for gentle terrain and easy landings.
Duna, Eve, Moho, Jool, EelooThe other planets of the stock Kerbol system, each with distinct atmosphere, gravity, and mission challenges covered on the Interplanetary Travel page.
EVAExtra-Vehicular Activity — a Kerbal outside their craft, on foot or using a jetpack.
IVAIntra-Vehicular Activity — the first-person view from inside a crewed part.
KSCKerbal Space Center — the home base where the VAB, SPH, and mission control live.
VABVehicle Assembly Building — where rockets are built.
SPHSpaceplane Hangar — where horizontally-launched craft are built.
KrakenThe community's nickname for physics-engine glitches that cause violent, uncommanded destruction.
AutostrutA part option that rigidly pins a part's position relative to another part (grandparent, heaviest, or root) without a visible strut.
Asparagus stagingA fuel-efficient staging pattern where outer boosters feed fuel inward to the core before being dropped, keeping the remaining stack closer to full.
Suicide burnA landing technique that delays the deceleration burn as late as physically possible to save fuel, timed precisely against current speed and altitude.
AerobrakeUsing atmospheric drag instead of fuel to slow down and capture into orbit.
Mainsail / Nerv / DawnExample stock engine names representing, respectively, a high-thrust liquid engine, a fuel-efficient nuclear thermal engine, and an extremely efficient low-thrust ion engine.
Mobile Processing LabA part that converts stored science data into additional Science points over time, and can clean up experiments for reuse.
ContractA Career-mode mission objective offered by KSC that pays funds, science, and/or reputation on completion.
ReputationA Career-mode resource reflecting public confidence in your space program, affecting contract offers and payouts.
StrategyA Career-mode policy you can adopt that trades one resource for another (for example, funds for science).
METMission Elapsed Time — the clock counting up from a vessel's launch, shown during flight.
